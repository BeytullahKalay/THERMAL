# THERMAL — Steam Store Description

## Tagline
**The night shift on Unit 4 will not save you. It might not even kill you. But it will hollow you out.**

## About This Game

You are OP.18, the latest night-shift operator at a Cold War–era thermal reactor. Your job is to keep three subsystems — **TEMPERATURE, PRESSURE, POWER** — inside their safe envelopes from 22:00 to 06:00. Your tools are a procedure manual, four manual valves, a sensor grid, a coffee mug, and the dim memory that someone sat in this chair before you.

The procedure manual is sometimes wrong.

You will not know which procedures lie. The cabinet at the back of the terminal holds encrypted log files written by **OP.14, OP.15, and OP.16**. They are not on the night shift anymore. None of them are. They left notes. The manual was not amended.

Between shifts you go home. You pay rent. You talk to Elena (or you don't). You watch what the facility does and does not say about the dead.

## Features

- **Real-time reactor monitoring** across three subsystems with continuous drift, anomaly cascades, and demand spikes
- **15+ cryptic SYSTEM ERROR codes (ER-XXXX)** — multiple are documented incorrectly. The truth is recoverable, if you have the time
- **Operator log archive** with a hack-decryption mini-game. Read what the operators before you wrote down before they vanished
- **Field-worker dispatch system** — radio calls with moral weight. The dose accumulates. Workers do collapse
- **Procedural dread** — alarm escalation, screen flicker, intermittent tremor, alarms that get louder until you answer them
- **Roguelike persistence** — your operator's final shift becomes a recoverable cache file in the next run's terminal archive. Whoever sits down next will read it
- **Full keyboard rebinding**, sound mixer, multiple display resolutions
- **Original CRT-ambient sound design** — every alarm, every spark, every clack of the relays is synthesised
- **3–6 hours per full playthrough · multiple endings · long-tail discoverability**

## What This Game Is

THERMAL is a slow horror about institutional indifference. It is a procedure-following game in which the procedures cannot be trusted. It is paperwork on a reactor floor.

It is also a job. Show up. Watch the gauges. Pay rent. Try not to think about the fact that the chair was empty when you got here, and that the chair will be empty again when you leave.

## What This Game Is Not

It is not action. It is not a roguelike in the build-a-deck sense. It is not a tutorial. The first hour is quiet on purpose. The fifth hour is not.
