## DESCRIPTION (Short)

THERMAL is a slow-horror reactor sim where the procedure manual is wrong on purpose. You play OP.18, the latest night-shift operator at a Cold War–era thermal facility. You follow the manual. The manual lies. The operators before you left margin notes. They are not on the night shift anymore. None of them are.

## DESCRIPTION (Long)

You are OP.18. Your job is the night shift on Unit 4 — 22:00 to 06:00. Three subsystems must stay inside their safe envelopes. Tools: a procedure manual, four valves, a sensor grid, a coffee mug.

The manual is sometimes wrong. You will not know which procedures lie until you have decrypted the operator log archive — files written by OP.14, OP.15, and OP.16. They left notes the manual was never amended to reflect.

Between shifts you go home. Pay rent. Read the news for what the facility does and does not say about its dead. Talk to Elena (or don't). Show up tomorrow.

When you fail — meltdown, eviction, exposure — the next operator who sits down at this terminal will find your final shift in their FILES tab. They will read what you accomplished. They will read what killed you. And they will start their own night shift.

## HISTORY (Why This Game Exists)

THERMAL was prompted by a single question: what if a video game's tutorial information was wrong on purpose, and the only way to find out was to stop trusting the system that gave it to you? The answer turned into a year of solo development and a small cast of dead operators who, between them, taught the player not to trust the facility that hires them.

## FEATURES

- Real-time monitoring of three independent reactor subsystems (TEMPERATURE, PRESSURE, POWER)
- 15+ cryptic SYSTEM ERROR codes; multiple intentionally documented incorrectly
- Operator log archive with a hack-decryption mini-game; truth is in the margins
- Field-worker dispatch: morally weighted radio decisions with persistent dose tracking
- Procedural dread — alarm escalation, screen flicker, intermittent reactor tremor
- Roguelike persistence: your operator's final shift becomes a recoverable file in your next run's archive
- ~3-6 hours per full playthrough · multiple endings
- Original CRT-ambient sound design, all synthesized in Web Audio
