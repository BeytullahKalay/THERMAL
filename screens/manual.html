SECTION 1
SYSTEM OVERVIEW
Unit 4 operates as a pressurised thermal loop facility under standard night rotation. The night shift operator assumes full responsibility for system monitoring between 22:00 and 06:00. No supervisory personnel are present on-site during this window.
The operator's role is observation and response. Unit 4 is a mature installation with a well-documented operational history. Under normal conditions, the systems are self-regulating. Operator intervention is required only when automated thresholds are exceeded.
OPERATOR RESPONSIBILITIES
01
Monitor temperature, pressure, and power output continuously throughout the shift.
02
Log all anomalies in the shift register. Do not skip entries.
03
Apply corrective action according to the procedures in Section 3.
04
Complete and submit the shift report before departing. Incomplete reports will be returned.
This manual reflects Unit 4's design parameters as of its last technical review. Operators are advised to follow the procedures as written. Deviation from procedure, even with good intention, increases system risk.

2.7 — SYSTEM ERROR CODES (ER-XXXX)
When a subsystem destabilises beyond automatic correction, the console issues an alphanumeric error code. While unresolved, the reactor degrades faster than normal. Press [H] to consult the procedure entry for the displayed code. Each fix is a four-condition lock: required POWER value, required PRESSURE value, valve sequence, and survey-tone sequence. All four must hold simultaneously.

SECTION 4
ANOMALY RESPONSE
Anomalies are defined as any reading or system state that falls outside the nominal ranges defined in Section 2. Operators should respond to anomalies promptly but without haste. Most anomalies in Unit 4 resolve with minimal intervention.
TEMPERATURE ANOMALY
01
Verify the reading is genuine. Wait 45 seconds if the system is otherwise stable. Instrumentation drift is common.
02
If confirmed elevated: allocate additional coolant resources. The Coolant Resonance Monitor should be adjusted to match target frequency.
03
If temperature exceeds 74°C: increase resource allocation and log the event. Expected resolution time: 60–90 seconds.
04
If temperature does not resolve within 3 minutes: escalate to facility management. Contact details are posted in the operator station.
PRESSURE ANOMALY
01
Pressure deviations above 57% typically self-correct. Observe for 60 seconds before acting.
02
If persistent: use the Valve Sequencing panel to restore nominal pressure flow.
03
Valve sequences are documented in the valve register. Follow the sequence exactly. Incorrect sequences may temporarily worsen the reading.
POWER OUTPUT ANOMALY
01
Power deviations above 84% are uncommon. Reduce power allocation immediately.
02
Do not over-correct. Reducing power too aggressively can trigger a secondary temperature response.
// IMPORTANT — FALSE ANOMALY CLASSIFICATION
Not every alert requires intervention. Unit 4 occasionally generates false anomaly signals due to sensor sensitivity calibrated for an earlier operational profile. Operators who respond to every alert indiscriminately will introduce more instability than they resolve. Learn to distinguish genuine anomalies from instrumentation artefacts. Experienced operators act on approximately 60–70% of alerts.
Anomaly procedures are based on Unit 4 design documentation and operational history.
Operators are reminded that escalation contacts are available at the operator station.
Response times listed are approximate. Actual resolution may vary.
SECTION 5
RADIATION MONITORING
Unit 4 operates within established radiation safety limits. The ambient radiation level in the operator station is monitored continuously. Operators should be aware of the following thresholds and the actions associated with each.
LEVEL READING REQUIRED ACTION
BACKGROUND 0.4 – 0.8 mSv No action required. Normal for this installation.
ELEVATED 0.8 – 1.2 mSv Log the reading. Monitor for increase. No immediate action.
NOTIFY 1.2 – 2.0 mSv Notify facility management at next available opportunity.
EVACUATE > 2.0 mSv Initiate evacuation protocol. Contact emergency line.
// OPERATOR HEALTH NOTE
Cumulative radiation exposure is tracked by facility health records. Operators are advised that short-duration exposure below 2.0 mSv presents no documented long-term health risk under current occupational guidelines. Annual health reviews are conducted for all night-shift personnel. Results are available on request through the personnel office.
The radiation monitor in your station displays a real-time reading. A reading above 1.2 mSv should be noted in your shift log. Readings above 2.0 mSv are rare under normal operating conditions. If the reading reaches this level, the cause is almost certainly an instrumentation fault rather than an actual release event.
Radiation thresholds conform to regional occupational exposure guidelines, Year 2 edition.

SECTION 6
EMERGENCY PROCEDURES
A critical system event is defined as any two or more parameters simultaneously in the critical range, or any single parameter that remains critical for more than three minutes without resolution. Critical events are rare in Unit 4's operational history.
IN THE EVENT OF A CRITICAL SYSTEM STATE
01
Do not leave the operator station. Your presence is required for system monitoring and documentation.
02
Apply corrective procedures from Section 3. Prioritise the parameter furthest from nominal.
03
Attempt to contact facility management using the emergency line posted at the operator station. If no answer, continue with procedure.
04
Document all actions taken in the shift log in real time. Accurate records are essential for post-event review.
05
If the event resolves: complete a full incident report before end of shift. If the event does not resolve: continue applying procedure.
// NOTE ON MELTDOWN CLASSIFICATION
A full thermal meltdown event has not occurred at Unit 4 under documented operational history. The term "meltdown" as used in other facility documentation refers to a specific threshold breach condition that the Unit 4 design is intended to prevent. Operators should understand that sustained correct procedure makes this outcome effectively impossible. The procedures in this manual are sufficient.
Operators who follow this manual's procedures will manage the full range of conditions that Unit 4 presents. No situation you will encounter during a normal night shift falls outside the scope of what is documented here. If you find that it does, the fault is in the documentation.
